## A

**Adhesives and sealants**

Any adhesive or sealant product used in an interior application, including exposed and concealed applications, and applied on site.

**Air changes**\
The measure of air volume added to or removed from a space.

**Air permeability**\
The measure of inward or outward air leakage through the building envelope.

**Apartment**\
A self-contained dwelling that occupies only part of a building.

**Applied coating**\
Any liquid-applied finish, including paints, varnishes, stains, and oils, used in an interior or exterior application.

**Average daylight factor (ADF)**\
The average ratio of the light level inside a structure to the light level outside the structure.

### B

**Brownfield development**\
A property development that previously contained at least one building with permanent foundations within the previous legal boundary, where the number of dwellings is no less than the number previously on site, and any previously cultivated or pastoral farmland contains no more than 50% of the total footprint.

**Building footprint (BF)**\
The area of land taken up by the permanent foundations of the dwelling or dwellings.

**Built Rating**\
The Homestar rating of a completed dwelling. A physical inspection by a Homestar Assessor is required.

### C

**CIBSE**\
Chartered Institution of Building Services Engineers.

**Climate zone**\
A designation of areas within New Zealand that share similar climatic characteristics.

**Competent professional**\
An individual with the specialised knowledge, technical aptitude, and experience required to carry out a task to an acceptable standard. This includes a formal qualification, or at least one year of experience, and a professional role relevant to the field in question.

**Conditioned floor area (CFA)**\
Space within the thermal envelope of the dwelling that could maintain a temperature band of 20–25°C for 365 days of the year.

**Continuous extract ventilation**\
A whole-dwelling ventilation system that extracts air continuously at a low rate.

### D

**Design Rating**\
The Homestar rating of a proposed dwelling based on detailed plans and specifications. It expires two years after issue.

**Drip line**\
The edge of a specimen or area of land located directly under the outer circumference of a shrub or tree’s branches. It is used to determine the percentage of site covered by native species.

### E

**EC**\
Electronically commutated. Electric motors with permanent magnets on the rotor and electronics to control the voltage and current applied to the motor.

### F

**Floor coverings**\
Coverings installed over the timber or concrete floor structure, including carpet, parquet, wood planks, laminate, tiles, and linoleum.

**fRsi**\
The temperature factor. It is the difference between the interior surface temperature and the exterior air temperature, divided by the average temperature difference between interior and exterior.

### G

**G-value**\
A measure of how much solar heat, or infrared radiation, is allowed through a glazing unit relative to no glass.

**Gross floor area (GFA)**\
Building footprint plus all areas of fully enclosed parts of the building built on top of the footprint, including internal and external walls.

### H

**Habitable area**\
The total internal floor area of bedrooms, lounge or living rooms, dining rooms, open plan kitchen areas, studies, and entertainment rooms.

### I

**IIC**\
Impact Insulation Class. The degree of soundproofing of impact noise in a floor or ceiling construction assembly.

### M

**Mixed-use residential development**\
A development that includes commercial or retail areas as well as residential areas within a single building.

**Multi-unit development**\
A development where multiple separate housing units are contained within one building or multiple buildings constructed on a site or complex within a defined boundary.

**MVHR**\
Mechanical ventilation with heat recovery. An energy recovery ventilation system that extracts stale air from a building while supplying fresh filtered air back in.

### N

**NRC**\
Noise Reduction Coefficient. The average sound absorption performance of a material.

### P

**Passive House**\
A voluntary standard and quality management system designed to achieve ultra-low energy buildings.

**Psi value**\
A measure of heat loss, or thermal bridging, within a junction of two thermal elements, measured in W/mK.

### R

**R-value**\
The thermal resistance rating used to determine a material or assembly’s ability to resist heat flow.

**Room-sealed appliance**\
An appliance designed so that air for combustion does not enter from, and combustion products do not enter into, the room in which the appliance is located, as defined in NZBC clause G4.

**Rw**\
Weighted Sound Reduction Index. A rating used to measure the level of sound insulation of walls, floors, windows, and doors, expressed in decibels (dB). Higher Rw values indicate better sound insulation.

### S

**sDA**\
Spatial Daylight Autonomy. A measure of how much of a space receives sufficient daylight.

**Shading**\
A measure of how much solar heat gain enters through a window compared to an unshaded window.

**Standalone home**\
A free-standing residential building with no intertenancy walls.

**STC**\
Sound Transmission Class. A rating of how well a partition in a building attenuates airborne sound.

**Stormwater detention**\
Systems designed to slow the rate of stormwater runoff.

**Stormwater retention**\
Systems designed to capture and store stormwater runoff, often for re-use as non-potable or potable water.

### T

**Terraced home**\
A dwelling with at least one common wall shared with another dwelling.

**Thermal envelope**\
The division between the outside area, which is unconditioned and usually uninsulated, and the inside area, which is conditioned and insulated.

**Typology**\
Dwellings with similar size, layout, and thermal properties.

### V

**Volume Certification**\
A pathway for awarding credit points using a standard specification with a quality assurance process to ensure adherence.

### W

**WELS**\
Water Efficiency Labelling Scheme. A scheme designed to provide information through labelling at the point of sale for consumers buying products that use water.

**Wet room**\
Rooms that are common sources of moisture in the home, including bathrooms, kitchens, and laundries.
